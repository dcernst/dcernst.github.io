<p class="ai-mathematics__meta">Terence Tao’s blog · 20 October 2026</p>
<p>Tao proposes a collaborative experiment: extract explicit upper bounds for a hypothetical tenth class-number-one discriminant from arguments concerning Siegel zeroes. Comparing bounds is intended to clarify the strengths of different approaches, including approaches that give poor bounds. Participants may use AI but must disclose it, provide a human-written summary, check outputs, and credit prior contributions. This is a research invitation and a model for responsible collaboration, not a report of a completed result.</p>

<article class="ai-mathematics__item" id="buckmaster-young-mathematicians">
<span class="ai-mathematics__tag">Student formation and research culture</span>
<h3><a href="https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/21/mathematics-is-a-subject-for-the-young/">Mathematics is a subject for the young</a></h3>
<p class="ai-mathematics__meta">Tristan Buckmaster, guest post on Terence Tao’s blog · 21 October 2026</p>
<p>Buckmaster argues that graduate students and postdocs should help shape mathematics as AI capabilities expand. He proposes stronger institutional protections for research data, investment in shared open infrastructure, and recognition for exposition, tools, ideas, and methods rather than priority alone. His proposals offer concrete questions for mentoring and evaluation policies. This is an expert’s perspective on the profession, not evidence that particular policies have already improved career outcomes.</p>
</article>
